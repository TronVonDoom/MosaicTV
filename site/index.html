Why MosaicTV exists

Cartoons air as the half-hours they were.

Plenty of classic cartoons were made as shorts: a half-hour was three seven-minute stories, or two with another show's short in the middle. Your files list each short as its own episode, so most tools air them as slivers, shuffled apart. MosaicTV puts them back together.

Group them once. They air together forever.

  • Suggest groupings packs a season's shorts into 11-, 22- or 30-minute slots to start from. Tick, group, reorder or ungroup from there.
  • Borrow a segment from another show — Dino Dudes aired with a Professor Penguin short between its two stories, so it does here too.
  • One program in the guide, one up-next card naming every part, and act breaks between the parts. No order or block ever splits it.
  • Nothing on disk changes. Your files keep their names and their real season and episode numbers.

Scheduling

Runs like a station. Edits like a playlist.

A 24/7 rotation, time blocks for the days and hours that are different, and a broadcast clock that starts shows on the :00 and :30. Change anything, any time — the guide follows from the next program, and every show carries on from the episode it was up to.

Channels › Mosaic Toons › Schedule

The Schedule tab warns you before it airs — a block that will run into an exact-time start, a collection too short for its blocks — and the clock shares each slot's break time out across the program's act breaks.

Blocks override the rotation for set days and times. A soft start waits for the program to finish; a hard start begins on the dot. Each block can have its own order, logo, breaks and up-next card.

Weeks ahead lays the schedule out a month at a time — what's on any day, and how late each block really starts. What aired keeps 90 days of history.

Collections are whole shows, seasons, episodes, movies, artists or albums — or a smart filter like “every horror movie”. Five playback orders, each explained as you pick it, with a preview of the first airings.

Station breaks

Breaks that look like a network made them.

Breaks cover the time between programs so blocks start on time. MosaicTV makes each channel's idents from its own logo, with a music bed — or plays your bumpers and old commercials from a folder, a fresh mix every break.

Commercial breaks where the commercials were.

  • Act breaks found for you — from a file's chapter marks, or from its fades to black and silence when it has none.
  • Breaks inside programs: on a clock, each slot's break time is shared across the episode's act breaks, the way it aired.
  • One place to set it up. Each channel's Breaks tab shows the next break and what it'll play, which blocks have breaks, and a week map of which ident plays when.
  • Per-block idents — a late-night look for the late-night block. Preview six seconds of any ident before you save.

On screen

An up-next card worth staying for.

Near the end of a program, a frosted-glass card slides in with what's next: its poster, the episode and its title, the year, genres, rating and the time it starts. Or pick a cable-style bar.

  • Glass or Broadcast, in any corner or along any edge, clear of your logo, in three sizes.
  • Stays on the picture — on a pillarboxed 4:3 show, the card and the logo sit inside the frame, not out in the black bars.
  • Music videos get a matching Now playing card.
  • A live preview in each channel's settings, with the channel's real next program.
Watch anywhere

Channel surfing, in a browser tab.

TV mode puts your channels full screen: a banner with what's on and next as you tune in, a guide over the picture, and the arrow keys or a swipe to flip. Cast it to a Chromecast or an Apple TV. Or add the channels to the player you already use.

Radio, too

Music channels with a now-playing screen.

Songs from your own files air over the album art, a spectrum drawn from the song, or its lyrics, lit line by line as they're sung.

  • Take turns by artist — a station of forty artists plays one song from each in turn, and shuffle never plays the same artist twice running.
  • Filed like a music app: artists, albums and songs by album artist, compilations under Various Artists, disc sets as one album.
  • MusicBrainz, Cover Art Archive and LRCLIB fill in albums, covers and synced lyrics — free, no keys.
  • What played before and what's next along the bottom, and music videos with their own Now playing card.

Choosing

MosaicTV and ErsatzTV

MosaicTV was inspired by ErsatzTV and rebuilt from scratch around a different idea: that a channel should look and feel like broadcast TV, and be simple to set up. Both are free and open source. Here's where they differ.

MosaicTV is for you if…

you want channels that feel like broadcast TV out of the box — real breaks, an up-next card, cartoons in their half-hours — set up by clicking rather than writing schedules.

ErsatzTV might suit you better if…

you want to schedule straight from a Plex, Jellyfin or Emby server, write scripted schedules, or run natively on Windows or macOS.

Install

On the air in ten minutes.

One container with everything in it. Point it at your media, open the web UI, and build your first channel.

  1. Run the container with your media mounted read-only — MosaicTV never writes to it.
  2. Add a library and scan it. Add a free TMDB key for posters and details if you like.
  3. Make a channel: a collection, a rotation, a number. Its guide builds itself.
  4. Watch in TV mode, or add the M3U and guide to your player.
LAN only. MosaicTV has no login — keep it on your home network, and use a VPN like Tailscale to watch away from home.
docker run -d \
  --name mosaictv \
  --restart unless-stopped \
  -p 8688:8688 \
  -e TZ=America/Chicago \
  -v /path/to/appdata/mosaictv:/app/data \
  -v /path/to/your/media:/media:ro \
  ghcr.io/tronvondoom/mosaictv:latest

Then open http://your-server:8688. Add --gpus all for an NVIDIA card.

services:
  mosaictv:
    image: ghcr.io/tronvondoom/mosaictv:latest
    container_name: mosaictv
    restart: unless-stopped
    ports:
      - "8688:8688"
    environment:
      - TZ=America/Chicago
    volumes:
      - /path/to/appdata/mosaictv:/app/data
      - /path/to/your/media:/media:ro
# On the Unraid terminal:
curl -L -o /boot/config/plugins/dockerMan/templates-user/my-MosaicTV.xml \
  https://raw.githubusercontent.com/TronVonDoom/mosaictv/main/unraid/my-MosaicTV.xml

Then Docker → Add Container → MosaicTV, point it at your media share and set your timezone.

:latest is the newest release; :edge follows every change. Full guides in the install docs.

Questions

Good to know

Is it really free?

Yes — every feature, for everyone, under the GPL-3.0. If it earns a spot on your server, you can sponsor it, but nothing is held back.

Will it change or move my files?

No. Mount your media read-only; MosaicTV only reads it. Broadcast episodes, matches and everything else live in its own database.

What hardware do I need?

Any 64-bit Intel or AMD machine that runs Docker — Unraid, a NAS with an Intel or AMD CPU, Proxmox, any Linux box. Only channels someone is watching are encoded, so a CPU copes with a viewer or two; a GPU makes light work of more. There's no ARM image yet.

Does it work with Plex?

Yes. MosaicTV includes an HDHomeRun tuner: enter its address in Plex's Live TV & DVR setup and Plex takes the channels and guide directly — no Threadfin or xTeVe in between.

Can I watch away from home?

Use a VPN into your home network, such as Tailscale or WireGuard. MosaicTV has no login, so don't expose its port to the internet.

Do I need TMDB or TheTVDB?

No. .nfo files and local artwork work on their own. A free TMDB or TheTVDB key adds posters, backdrops, cast and episode details for everything else.

Where do these screenshots come from?

A demo library made for this site: every show, movie, band and commercial here is made up, with artwork and music generated for it. The screens are MosaicTV 0.14 running on that library.
